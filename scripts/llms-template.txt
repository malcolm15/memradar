# MemRadar

MemRadar tracks retail prices for <!--PRODUCT_COUNT--> RAM and SSD products and keeps the history, so a shopper can tell a fair price from a bad one instead of guessing. Price history reaches back to <!--EARLIEST_YEAR--> for the longest-tracked products and is licensed from Keepa; current prices are compared across Amazon and Newegg, and the whole catalog is re-checked several times a day. The Memory Price Index aggregates that history into median price changes per segment (DDR5, DDR4, NVMe SSD, SATA SSD) over 1 month, 3 months, 6 months and 1 year, recomputed daily. Everything on the site is derived from recorded observations: there are no review scores, no star ratings, and no editorial claims that the data does not support.

## Key pages

- [Homepage](https://memradar.com/): Search the catalog, see the Market Pulse summary of segment price changes, and the largest current price drops.
- [RAM](https://memradar.com/ram/): All tracked DDR5 and DDR4 kits with live prices, filterable by type, capacity, speed and brand.
- [SSDs](https://memradar.com/ssd/): All tracked NVMe and SATA drives with live prices, filterable by type, form factor, capacity and brand.
- [Memory Price Index](https://memradar.com/price-index/): Median price change per segment across four time windows, recomputed daily from the full price history.
- [Data for journalists](https://memradar.com/data/): Current findings, how to cite, catalog composition, and the downloadable files below.

- [Will RAM prices go back down?](https://memradar.com/blog/will-ram-prices-go-back-down/): What the last memory price cycle looked like in our own data, 2016 to 2020.
- [Guides](https://memradar.com/guides/): Buying guides argued from the tracked price data rather than opinion.
- [Should I Buy RAM Now?](https://memradar.com/guides/should-i-buy-ram-now/): What the 2025 to 2026 memory price surge did to DDR5 and DDR4, and how to judge a fair kit price today.
- [Should I Buy an SSD Now?](https://memradar.com/guides/should-i-buy-an-ssd-now/): A decade of falling storage prices, what changed in late 2025, and how to buy a drive well in the current market.
- [Spec glossary](https://memradar.com/glossary/): Plain-English definitions of every RAM and SSD specification MemRadar reads off a product listing.
- [Product pages](https://memradar.com/sitemap.xml): One page per tracked product, each with full price history, all-time low and high, a monthly and yearly price table, dated price milestones, and a per-gigabyte comparison against similar products.

## Notes for reuse

- Price figures change daily. A page's own "last updated" line states when its figures were computed.
- Figures over longer windows rest on smaller, older product sets, so quote long-window percentages as magnitudes rather than to a decimal.
- Product pages with under <!--MIN_DAYS_INDEXABLE--> days of tracked history carry noindex and are excluded from the sitemap; they hold too little history to be worth citing.
- Amazon and Newegg links are affiliate links. Price history is Amazon data licensed from Keepa. Newegg data comes from Rakuten Advertising.

## Data for developers

Two files are public and free to use with attribution:

- /data/raycast-v1-market.json - median price and median price per gigabyte for each segment (DDR5, DDR4, NVMe, SATA), with the change over one month, three months, six months and a year and the number of products each was measured across. Regenerated daily and carries the date it was computed. The version sits in the path, so this file keeps its shape and any change ships as a new version beside it.
- /data/memradar-price-index-monthly.csv - monthly price levels per segment, back to <!--CSV_FIRST_MONTH--> on the longest series, with the product count behind every row. Regenerated daily. Read the # comment lines at the top of the file before computing any change from it, because a change computed between two rows is not the same measure as the Price Index.

Attribution to MemRadar is required when you use either file: Source: MemRadar Memory Price Index, memradar.com/price-index/

Per-product price history is not available for third-party use. The history behind these figures is licensed from Keepa, and only these aggregate files are published for reuse.
