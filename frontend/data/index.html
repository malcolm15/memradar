Memory Price Data for Journalists and Researchers

MemRadar tracks retail prices for 235 consumer RAM and SSD products, with recorded history reaching back to 2015 on the longest-tracked parts. The data is free to cite with attribution, and custom cuts are available on request at no cost. This page exists so you can decide quickly whether it is useful for a story.

Contact: hello@memradar.com.

Tracked days counts distinct days on which a price was recorded for that product, so a median of 200 means a typical product in that segment carries roughly 200 recorded days of history.

Prices are what a consumer pays at retail in the United States, recorded from Amazon (history, licensed from Keepa) and Newegg (current prices, via the retailer's catalog feed). This is a different series from the contract and spot prices quoted in industry reporting, and it follows those with a lag of weeks.

Two JSON files serve the same data to software rather than to people: segment trends and per-product prices with monthly history, both regenerated daily, each carrying the date it was computed.

The monthly series is available as a CSV download, giving the median price, the median price per gigabyte and the product count for each segment and month; read the notes at the top of the file before computing any change from it.

Amazon prices are recorded six times a day. The full definitions, calculations, and limitations are on the methodology page, including what this data cannot tell you. That page is worth a skim before citing a figure.

Current findings

Figures below are computed from tracked prices and update automatically. Each is stated as a magnitude rather than to a decimal place, because comparisons over longer windows rest on smaller product sets and can shift as the catalog changes. The exact current numbers are on the price index.

  • The median DDR5 memory kit costs more than four times what it cost a year ago. Computed September 20, 2026.
  • Storage followed memory up: NVMe and SATA solid state drives have both more than doubled year over year. Computed September 20, 2026.
  • 206 of the 235 products MemRadar tracks, 88%, currently sell for at least one and a half times their lowest recorded price. Computed September 21, 2026.
  • 155 of the 235 tracked products sell for more than three times their lowest recorded price. Computed September 21, 2026.

How to cite

Suggested attribution:

Source: MemRadar Memory Price Index, memradar.com/price-index/

Please include the date the figure was computed, which appears on every page carrying data. Figures change daily.

You are welcome to reproduce charts and tables from this site with attribution. If you need a higher-resolution version of a chart, ask.

Charts to download

Five charts of the monthly series, free to publish with attribution. Each is 1200 by 675 pixels and carries its source and the month its data runs through inside the image, so the credit survives if a caption is dropped.

Embed the dated file, not the latest one. A dated file never changes once published. The latest file is replaced each month when a new month of data completes, so an article embedding it would find its chart changing under text written about the old one. Earlier months stay at their own addresses.

  • DDR5 memory: median price per GB, data through August 2026. Always the newest month: ddr5-latest.png
  • DDR4 memory: median price per GB, data through August 2026. Always the newest month: ddr4-latest.png
  • NVMe SSDs: median price per drive, data through August 2026. Always the newest month: nvme-latest.png

Memory is charted per gigabyte because the typical kit size shifts from month to month. SSDs are charted per drive because their price per gigabyte moves with the mix of drive sizes. These are price levels: for how much prices changed over a period, cite the price index, which compares each product with itself.

Custom data requests

Most of what is useful for a story is not on the site. If you tell us what you are writing about, we can usually produce it the same day at no cost. Examples of what has been asked for or is readily available:

  • A segment's median price by month over any period we cover.
  • The price history of a specific product, as a series rather than a chart.
  • Which tracked products are furthest from, or closest to, their all-time lows.
  • A comparison of two segments over a matched window, using the same fairness rule the index applies.
  • The distribution of current prices against all-time lows across the catalog.

We do not charge for this and do not ask for anything in return beyond attribution. Write to hello@memradar.com with the question you are trying to answer and a deadline if you have one.

What we can and cannot speak to

Worth stating plainly so you know whether to call.

We can speak to: what consumer retail prices have actually done over the period we cover, how specific products and segments have moved, how current prices compare with their own history, and how our figures are calculated.

We cannot speak to: contract or spot pricing, manufacturer production decisions, supply forecasts, or anything about the memory industry's internals. Those are TrendForce's and the manufacturers' territory, and we would be guessing.

We are a single-person project with a licensed data source, not a research firm. That is worth knowing before you describe us in print.
