What is in each basket

This is a shopping basket, not an index. Each component is the median price across the products we tracked in that exact specification that month, and the basket is their sum. Nothing is weighted and nothing is rebased.

  • Current build: a 32GB DDR5-6000 kit, a 1TB NVMe drive and a 2TB NVMe drive.
  • DDR4 build: a 32GB DDR4-3200 kit and a 1TB NVMe drive.

The specifications are fixed. They are not the cheapest parts available, and they are not a recommendation: they are a constant basket, so that a change in the figure is a change in prices rather than a change in what is being priced.

The series

Each line is the basket total at the end of that month. The current month never appears, because a partial month moves under the reader for no reason other than how far through it you are. A month's figure is fixed once the month ends and is not revised afterwards.

What this does not tell you

The series starts in , and that is a limit of the catalog rather than of the market. A basket can only begin when every one of its components has enough tracked products to give an honest median, which we set at five. The 1TB NVMe component is the binding one: it reaches five products in January 2023, later than any other component in either basket. There are earlier figures, but they sit above a gap rather than continuing a run, so they are not published.

Capacities are not pooled to buy more history. Merging 1TB and 2TB drives into one component would push both baskets back to March 2022. It is not done, because the component would stop being "a 1TB drive" and become "a drive", and the basket would no longer describe a build anyone would assemble.

The year-over-year figure swings hard, and it says as much about the month it is measured against as the month it is measured in. For the current build it has run from -20.1% in January 2024 to +210.7% in May 2026, measured across the published monthly series, moving by tens of points from one month to the next. The dollar figure is the durable claim here; the percentage is the volatile one, which is why it is published as a floor rather than a decimal.

These are regular Amazon listing prices, not deals. Lightning Deals, Prime-exclusive member prices and Warehouse prices are not in our data at all, so this says nothing about what any sale offered. Prices are the Amazon price, or the best marketplace price where Amazon has no offer of its own.

How this relates to the Price Index

The Memory Price Index compares each product with itself over a period, which is the right way to ask how much prices moved. This page adds up what a fixed set of parts cost, which is the right way to ask what a build costs. A change computed between two rows here will not equal the Price Index for the same period, and the two answer different questions.
