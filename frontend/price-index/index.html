What this measures. For each product with at least 20 days of price coverage in both months, we take its median daily price in September 2026 and compare it with its own median daily price in September 2025. The figure is the middle value across those products. Each comparison follows one listing (the same Amazon ASIN) across both months, so products launched in between are left out rather than mixed in.

History covers only products in MemRadar's catalog, which was assembled in July 2026; products discontinued before then are not represented. How the record is built, and what it cannot tell you.

Methodology

The monthly record at the top is the index's year-over-year figure. It compares each listing with itself a year earlier, using the median of its daily prices in each month. For months after the launch series, a record is frozen after its month ends, once a price fetch timestamped after the month boundary has landed, and is never recomputed; the launch series from October 2024 to September 2026 was reconstructed from stored price history and frozen on October 12, 2026. A correction is listed with its date rather than rewriting the figure. The live market reading and the summary below cover the short-period figures.

The summary below covers the live reading. How MemRadar tracks prices is the full reference: where the data comes from, how every figure is calculated, and what it cannot tell you.

Every figure is a median, not an average. A single mispriced listing at $1,900 drags a mean badly in a segment of thirty products; the median reports the typical price and stays stable as the catalog changes.

Each period compares the same set of products in both timeframes. A product only counts toward a period if we have both a current price and a price from that period's baseline window, and both medians are computed over that same subset. Products added recently cannot distort a one-year figure by appearing on only one side of it. Subset sizes differ between periods for this reason, and that is the honest result rather than a flaw.

Figures over longer windows rest on smaller, older product sets, since a product only counts if we tracked it that far back. As the catalog grows, a long-window figure can move when its cohort changes, independently of any price movement.

Amazon price history is licensed from Keepa; Newegg pricing comes from an official Rakuten Advertising product feed. Prices refresh every few hours and the live reading is normally recomputed once a day. The catalog holds 235 products, 119 memory and 116 storage, with the deepest price history running back to 2015.

Citing this data

This page has two kinds of Price Index figures, and each should be cited differently.

The monthly record, the year-over-year figure: use the source line or the formal reference in the record's citation box above. Both name the record and link its permanent page, and the formal reference carries the date it was frozen, so the figure can be checked after the next record is published.

The live market reading, the one-, three- and six-month figures: attribute it as the live reading, with the date it was computed, since it is recomputed and the figures move.
